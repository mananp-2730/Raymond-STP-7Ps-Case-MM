One mill, a hundred years, and a new wardrobe of businesses

Raymond was incorporated in 1925 as a woollen mill in Thane. It grew into the brand behind one of Indian advertising's longest-running ideas, "The Complete Man." By its centenary year, the group had split into three separate listed companies. This project follows Raymond Lifestyle — the fashion arm now trying to become a complete lifestyle brand, rather than a supplier of suiting fabric.

01The cut

Splitting one company into three

Between 2024 and 2025, Raymond separated into three independently listed companies. Chairman Gautam Singhania framed the split as creating three distinct growth engines in place of one conglomerate. Raymond Lifestyle took over the apparel, fashion, and retail business on 30 June 2024. Raymond Realty — whose revenue grew 45% to ₹2,313 crore in FY25 — was demerged on 1 May 2025 and listed on 1 July 2025. What remains of Raymond Limited is now a pure engineering company, making tools, auto components, and, following its Maini Precision acquisition, aerospace parts.

03Positioning

From the complete man to a complete life

Since 1992, Raymond's brand equity was built on one campaign: a quietly confident, family-oriented man in a Raymond suit. The repositioning challenge is to stretch that trust from "he wears a great suit" to "his whole life carries the Raymond name" — without breaking the equity that got the brand here.

04Product planning

Cutting new patterns

Suiting and shirting fabric is Raymond's mature core — the company holds roughly 65% share of India's branded worsted-suiting segment and still treats it as the anchor of the group. Growth is being asked of the newer patterns instead: Ethnix's festive wear, Raymond Home, and a new sleepwear line, Sleepz by Raymond, alongside innerwear under Park Avenue. Each is a deliberate line extension riding on decades of fabric credibility.

06Integration

Where the threads meet

None of this works as separate slides. Segmentation by occasion is why Raymond built four different store formats. Those formats only make sense once positioning is stretched from one man's suit to a household's whole life. That wider positioning is what justifies spending on Ethnix, Home, and Sleepz instead of just making more suits. And the marketing mix — tiered pricing, a more selectively-grown store network, and a GST-linked price move — exists to deliver each segment the version of "complete lifestyle" it actually came in for.
